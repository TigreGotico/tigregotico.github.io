## Wakewords are the front door

A voice assistant's first task is **wakeword detection** — hearing "hey assistant" and waking up. Most commercial assistants use proprietary, closed-source detectors. If you want to build a voice system, you either use their wakeword or build your own from scratch.

We published **seven original synthetic wakeword datasets** generated by TigreGotico representing common assistant names:

- **[hey_computer](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TigreGotico/synthetic-wakeword-hey_computer)**
- **[hey_mycroft](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TigreGotico/synthetic-wakeword-hey_mycroft)**
- **[hey_siri](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TigreGotico/synthetic-wakeword-hey_siri)**
- **[alexa](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TigreGotico/synthetic-wakeword-alexa)**
- **[home_assistant](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TigreGotico/synthetic-wakeword-home_assistant)**
- **[voice_assistant](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TigreGotico/synthetic-wakeword-voice_assistant)**
- **[wake_up](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TigreGotico/synthetic-wakeword-wake_up)**

Each dataset contains:
- Positive examples (the wakeword spoken multiple ways)
- Negative examples (similar-sounding phrases that should NOT trigger)
- Metadata (speaker, accent, noise level)

**All original: fully synthetic, generated by TigreGotico** from our **[phoonnx](https://github.com/TigreGotico/phoonnx)** TTS framework with **Miro and Dii voices**.

## Why synthetic wakewords win over real recordings

Recording real wakewords seems obvious — just get a microphone and record. But that path is full of friction:

- **Slow**: Recording, consenting, organizing takes months. Synthetic takes hours.
- **Privacy-fraught**: You need consent forms from every speaker. Data lives in your database. GDPR compliance is a headache.
- **Coverage-incomplete**: You recorded 100 people. What about accents from regions you didn't cover? Different speaking speeds? Noisy environments?
- **Hard to replicate**: Someone wants to train their own detector. They can't retrace your recordings.

**Synthetic wakewords sidestep all of this:**

- **Fast**: Generate 10,000 variations in an afternoon using **[phoonnx](https://github.com/TigreGotico/phoonnx)** TTS
- **Diverse**: Vary speaker gender, age, accent, speaking speed, background noise — all programmatically
- **Auditable**: The generation script is the documentation. Anyone can see what data was created and why.
- **Reproducible**: Same seed, same speaker, same wakeword → same audio every time. No randomness, full control.
- **Private**: No humans involved, no recordings, no consent forms, no privacy liability.

The tradeoff: synthetic audio sounds slightly less natural than recordings. But for wakeword detection (which doesn't need perfect naturalness, just acoustic distinctiveness), synthetic is **better**.
