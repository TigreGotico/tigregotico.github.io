## Games for people who cannot see

When blind players want to game, the options are sparse. Text adventures exist. Screen reader games exist. But true interactive audio games — where the interface *is* the audio, not an afterthought — are rare.

We are building them. Our philosophy: blind players deserve the same story depth, challenge, and agency that sighted players get. That means bespoke audio design, not generic voiceovers.

## The games we are building

**[tower_of_silence](https://github.com/voice_games/tower_of_silence)** is our flagship. You are alone in a tower. You hear footsteps above. Doors open and close. Wind howls. You navigate by sound, solve puzzles by ear, and face enemies that move through 3D space — all audio.

Other titles in the suite:
- **tnalagmes** — a family of games covering different genres
- **[classic games remakes](https://github.com/voice_games/)** — audio versions of known titles

## Why audio-only design matters

A sighted game hides information in visuals: a red health bar, a minimap, a distant sprite. A blind player cannot see those signals.

Audio-only games do not hide. They *communicate* through sound. A nearby enemy has footsteps getting louder. Low health is a heartbeat accelerating. You are far from home when the wind gets quieter.

The difference: sighted design is a map. Audio design is immersion.

## The tools

Our games use **[phoonnx](https://github.com/TigreGotico/phoonnx)** for voice synthesis, giving blind players dialogue from Miro and Dii — two consistent voice identities they hear across all our games.

## Why now

Accessibility is not charity. Blind gamers are players. They deserve games built for their senses, not games that were designed sighted and retrofitted with audio. That requires:

1. **Full audio design** — from music to spatial cues to player feedback
2. **Open code** — so blind developers can extend and remix
3. **Consistent voices** — **[Miro & Dii](/blog/2026-06-15-two-voices-every-language-miro-and-dii)** are recognized across the whole suite
